## How we work

Philosophy: Premium products deserve a process that matches. Craft, not templates. No generic playbooks. No documentation-first delivery.

Every phase is designed to create clarity, momentum, and a product the client is proud to launch.

The same visual rigor in the portfolio is how client work is built. Design and engineering happen in the same rhythm: flows are validated with real interfaces, architecture is refined alongside UX, and work ships in increments so momentum does not stall.

### Phase 01 — Discover (Research)

We immerse in the product vision, users, and constraints, mapping the problem before writing code.

Outcome of this principle: strategic discovery. Every engagement starts with clarity — user research, constraints, and a shared definition of success.

In practice this has looked like discovery calls, requirement deep-dives, reverse-engineering a founder's Excel, and sitting inside an existing operational workflow (recruitment, farm collection, insurance calls) before proposing architecture.

### Phase 02 — Design (Architecture)

Interfaces, flows, and architecture are shaped with the same care brought to the studio's own products.

Outcome: product-grade UX. Interfaces and flows designed to feel intentional, premium, and effortless at every touchpoint.

Recurring design choices across projects:

- Separate surfaces for each kind of user (super admin, operator, end customer) instead of one overloaded dashboard
- Role-based access that matches how the organization actually works
- Visual builders and guided workflows so non-engineers can operate the product
- Public, login-free experiences where the end user should not have to create an account (QR product passports)

### Phase 03 — Build (Execution)

Full-stack execution across AI, web, and cloud, shipped in tight loops with relentless attention to detail.

Outcome: full-stack execution. From architecture to deployment, cohesive systems, not handoffs.

Stack patterns that recur:

- Web platforms: React front ends, Node.js or Django/Django REST Framework backends, PostgreSQL, AWS, Docker
- AI voice: Python, FastAPI, WebSockets, OpenAI, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, and swappable STT/LLM/TTS providers
- AI product features: OCR, RAG, vector databases, natural-language querying over a client's own data
- Auth and access: JWT, OAuth, role-based access control, multi-organization isolation
- Payments and usage: Stripe, token wallets, usage-based monetization

### Phase 04 — Deliver (Launch)

Production-ready systems with the polish, performance, and reliability premium products demand.

Outcome: launch and iteration. Production-ready delivery, then continued refinement.

What "delivered" has meant on real projects: live revenue (Healthy Fasal), pilot-ready MVP (DigiProPass), measurable operational reduction inside a 2,000-person enterprise (Natvoiz at Natsoft), a backend reused across multiple client organizations (Setoo, BU Bhandari, Vani Connect), and a productized version of a paid consulting method (Axion Plan).

### What a client gets

- Strategic discovery
- Product-grade UX
- Full-stack execution
- Launch and iteration

End-to-end craft across every layer. From strategy to shipped product: one team, one standard, one cohesive experience.

### DigiProPass

Digital Product Passport platform for sustainable products.

A web platform that helps brands comply with upcoming European regulations requiring Digital Product Passports (DPP) for consumer products. It sits between regulatory compliance, brand transparency, and consumer trust. A shopper scans a QR code and sees sustainability details, origin, materials, and environmental impact scores. No login.

Live: https://digipropass.com/

Year: 2024. Duration: 6 months. Role on the engagement: full stack. Industry: sustainability. Category: web platform.

Stack: Django, Django REST Framework, PostgreSQL, AWS, Docker, JWT, OAuth.

Client: Keishika Mahesh, Founder and CEO of DigiProPass. She found BitBLabs on LinkedIn. A discovery call and requirement deep-dive turned the idea into a working web application with a technical and business roadmap.

The problem. The European Union is moving toward mandatory Digital Product Passports for products sold in the region. Most brands cannot track sustainability data at product level, keep ingredient and origin transparency, or generate and manage QR passports at scale. DigiProPass is the centralized system that creates, manages, and distributes those passports.

Who it is for.

- Business users: consumer-goods brands, manufacturers, exporters
- Consumers: people who scan the code
- Secondary users: compliance teams, product managers, sustainability officers

Architecture. Three layers, kept separate:

- Super admin panel, used internally by DigiProPass for platform management
- Business dashboard, used by brands and manufacturers to manage products
- Public passport view, used by consumers after a scan

Business dashboard. Self-service onboarding, CSV bulk import, product editing with sustainability data, and multiple passports linked to one product. Real-time metrics include CO₂ emissions, water usage, and eco scores.

Access control, sized for different organizations:

- Admin: full access, including product creation, passport generation, and data editing
- Manager: day-to-day operations and ingredient management
- Custom roles: planned, so organizations can add specialized permissions later

Product management. Products are added one by one or in bulk via CSV. Each product can be categorized, tagged, and filled with materials, ingredients, and country of origin. Filtering is category-based.

Sustainability scoring engine.

1. Brands input ingredients and materials.
2. They set the percentage contribution of each ingredient.
3. A proprietary algorithm processes that data.
4. The product receives an overall sustainability score.

Digital passports. Once product data is complete, a brand can generate multiple passports per product. Each passport has a unique URL and QR code, so traceability can be per unit or per batch. The dashboard tracks active passports, total quantities, and QR distribution.

Consumer experience. The scan lands on a product page: description, material composition, care instructions, recyclability, and circular services. Instant, at the point of purchase, without an account.

Status. Core platform designed and built. MVP implementation complete. Ready for pilot brand onboarding. Planned next: Shopify and WooCommerce integrations, deeper analytics, regulatory exports.

Differentiators.

- Aligned with upcoming EU compliance requirements
- Product-level transparency, not only brand-level claims
- QR passport generation that scales
- Clean split between admin, business, and consumer
- Sustainability scoring inside the product workflow, not bolted on later

Stated metrics: 10,000+ products tracked, 100% compliance rate, 50+ brand partners. The narrative slides also describe the build as MVP-complete and ready for pilots, so the larger counts should be treated as the product's stated scale targets alongside a finished core.

### Healthy Fasal

Zero-inventory farm-to-vendor grocery supply platform.

A procurement and distribution platform that simplifies the farm-to-vendor supply chain: freshness, less wastage, better margins. Fresh produce is bought only when demand exists, then delivered to vendors without the platform holding inventory.

Live: https://healthyfasal.com/

Year: 2025. Duration: 4 months. Role: full stack. Industry: AgriTech. Category: web platform.

Stack: React, Node.js, PostgreSQL, Express, AWS, Docker.

Client: Mr. Kamlesh, a farmer who also owns a logistics business. The project was referred through a trusted network. His combination of farming and logistics is what made the supply-chain failures obvious to him.

The problem. Groceries reach vendors late and stale because of inventory mismanagement. Middle layers add delay, waste, and weak quality control. Vendors have no structured way to order. Farmers are disconnected from real demand.

The principle: zero inventory. Demand first, procurement second. Vendors place orders. Procurement happens only after demand is confirmed. Farmers supply fresh produce. Collection centers aggregate and dispatch. No pre-stocking.

Architecture. Four operational fronts, built to scale city by city without redesign:

- Super admin panel: central command for multi-city operations
- Collection center panel: procurement and dispatch
- Vendor panel: ordering for grocery shop owners
- Sales panel: on-ground vendor onboarding

Super admin can create and manage collection centers across cities, control areas and routing, approve vendors, monitor orders, payments, and delivery, and set platform-wide configuration.

Product catalog. Pricing tiers depend on shop type: canteen, outlet, franchise, restaurant. Each product shows cost price and selling prices and can be managed across collection centers. The point of the tiers is fair margins for each kind of buyer.

Vendor experience. Vendors browse products, see prices in their local language, and check out against a wallet balance. Wallet credits remove payment delays.

Order flow.

1. Vendor places an order using wallet balance.
2. The order routes to the assigned collection center.
3. The center procures fresh produce from farmers.
4. Packing lists, challans, and invoices are generated, then the order is dispatched.
5. Delivery is marked complete.

Order management gives admins status filters, payment tracking, multi-center support, and counts for total, confirmed, dispatched, and delivered orders.

Sales workflow, built for grocery distribution where relationships are physical:

1. Sales visits local shops.
2. They explain the model and collect leads and feedback.
3. Interested vendors are submitted.
4. Super admin validates and approves.
5. Credentials go out and the vendor orders digitally.

Zero-inventory outcomes claimed: near-zero wastage, fully fresh produce, no capital locked in stock, and farmer supply aligned to actual orders.

Traction claimed: more than $120K monthly turnover (USD), live in production, multiple collection centers, 1,000+ orders processed. The site copy calls this a working revenue-generating system, not a concept.

Why it is in the portfolio: a ground-level problem, a founder with domain depth, operational logic that matches the software, a model that reduces inventory risk, and revenue that validates it.

### Natvoiz AI Voice Bot

Enterprise-grade, configurable, low-latency AI voice automation.

An end-to-end AI voice platform for enterprise-scale conversations across recruitment, insurance, sales, and customer support. Built for Natsoft, a US-based consulting enterprise with BPO operations and more than 2,000 employees.

Related live reference: https://natsoft.com/

Year: 2023. Duration: 8 months. Role: AI engineer and full stack. Industry: voice AI. Category: AI product.

Stack: Python, FastAPI, OpenAI, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, WebSockets, React, PostgreSQL.

The original job. Replace repetitive, high-volume human calling with AI conversations that still feel human, and send structured results back to the CRM.

Natsoft's recruitment problem: recruiters buried in repetitive follow-ups, candidates dropping off after offers, no structured capture of why people reject offers, and manual follow-ups that created delay, messy data, and burnout. They needed screening, automatic follow-up, and insights in the CRM.

What was proposed. Not a single-purpose bot. A configurable voice orchestration platform that HR, operations, and business teams can adapt to new workflows without engineering. That decision is what later opened insurance, sales, and support on the same system.

Visual flow builder. Drag-and-drop conversation nodes, prompts and responses, logic paths, fallbacks, and multi-step call flows. Built for non-engineers.

Voice and AI stack, configurable per flow:

- Speech-to-text: Deepgram, Google STT, AssemblyAI, Gladia
- Language models: OpenAI GPT models, Google LLMs
- Text-to-speech: ElevenLabs, Google TTS, with voice and tone settings
- Real-time intent detection and sentiment
- RAG against SOPs, FAQs, and policy documents
- Each flow picks the providers that fit that use case

Voice flows. Multiple flows live side by side: offer-acceptance calls in English, Telugu, and Kannada, insurance flows, and others. Flows can be duplicated, edited, and tracked on their own. One platform, many languages and jobs.

CRM loop.

1. An offer letter released in the CRM triggers the voice bot.
2. The bot places the outbound call.
3. It confirms status, explains next steps, and collects feedback.
4. Recordings, transcripts, and sentiment sync back to the CRM.

Analytics. Every call stores LLM latency, TTS latency, hangup reason, and status. Date filters and one-click transcripts. A live conversation view shows timestamped messages and per-response latency.

Latency, described as the hardest technical win. Initial end-to-end latency was about 2 seconds. It was brought to about 300 milliseconds, roughly an 85% reduction, through streaming pipelines, async orchestration, optimized model chaining, and infrastructure tuning. The portfolio metric line is "~300ms".

After each call the system produces a transcript, structured feedback, sentiment, intent tags, a stored recording, and a CRM sync.

Impact at Natsoft:

- 35–45% reduction in manual follow-ups
- Faster decision cycles
- Structured capture of rejection reasons (stated as full feedback capture)
- A consistent experience at scale
- Recruiters moved onto higher-value work

Portfolio metrics: ~300ms latency, 500+ call logs, 8+ voice flows.

Enterprise features:

- Multiple organizations with isolated data and flows
- Role-based access to flows, analytics, and logs
- Knowledge base and RAG so answers stay inside policy
- Audit trail of recordings and transcripts

What it became. A recruitment tool that turned into an enterprise voice platform, deployed across recruitment, insurance, sales, and customer support.

### Axion Plan

AI-powered financial forecasting and business planning.

A B2C platform for enterprise leaders to model revenue, costs, profitability, and cash flow without living in complex spreadsheets. It productizes a forecasting method that already made money in Excel.

Live: https://axionplan.com/

Year: 2025. Duration: 5 months. Role: full stack and AI engineer. Industry: FinTech. Category: AI product.

Stack: React, Node.js, PostgreSQL, OpenAI, OCR, RAG, Stripe.

Client: Dr. Steven, a Malaysia-based investment banker and executive coach who works with C-suite leaders. He brought investment banking experience, executive coaching, and Excel models clients were already paying for. The spreadsheets worked. They did not scale past 1:1 engagements, they were hard for non-finance people, they were poor for team collaboration, and they had no AI explanations.

Why the build was hard. The domain is real finance: profit-and-loss statements, balance sheets, cash flow logic, revenue modeling. The team came from software, so the work required repeated deep-dives, hard questions, and reverse-engineering Excel formulas until the software matched the model. The studio describes it as a commerce education as much as a product build.

Architecture. Two fronts:

- Super admin, where Dr. Steven manages users, usage, and settings, so the platform can be run as a business
- Company panel, where an organization creates projects, enters data, and generates forecasts

What a company can do:

- Input and track revenue streams
- Define cost structures: direct, indirect, fixed, variable
- Distribute figures by month
- Generate profit-before-tax and profitability forecasts
- Analyze cash position over time
- Model target-based scenarios

Workflows follow real financial thinking, expressed as guided screens instead of spreadsheet formulas.

AI, used as augmentation of the model rather than a gimmick:

- OCR ingestion of P&L statements, with automatic cost categorization
- Questions such as "Why is profit dropping in month 4?"
- Natural-language queries over the project's own numbers
- Answers written for executives, not for analysts
- What-if questions such as "What happens if revenue increases 15%?"

Monetization is usage-based, on tokens:

1. Each user has a token wallet.
2. Tokens are spent on project creation, AI insights, and OCR.
3. Wallets can be topped up or covered by a subscription.
4. Price tracks value delivered instead of a flat seat fee.

Stripe is part of the stack for this commercial layer.

Roles, for real company collaboration:

- Owner: organization and billing
- Analyst: create and analyze models
- Editor: edit data and projections
- Viewer: read-only reports

Support. A RAG chatbot answers questions about how to use the platform, financial concepts, and features, grounded in internal documentation. If confidence is low, it sends the user to WhatsApp support. The aim is less support load and a better first answer.

Business result claimed:

- Consulting expertise turned into a product
- Faster client onboarding
- Manual Excel workflows removed from the critical path
- The practice can scale past 1:1 coaching
- Non-finance leaders understand the numbers better

Portfolio metrics are qualitative: FinTech domain, enterprise users, 3+ AI features.

Why it is in the portfolio: non-technical domain depth, financial logic translated accurately into software, AI doing real work on the client's data, and UX, business model, and AI pointed at the same outcome.

### Course Companion

Privacy-first AI teaching assistant for universities and professors.

A platform for universities, professors, and academic institutions to use AI in teaching without giving up data security, intellectual property, or institutional policy. It is meant to be a digital extension of a specific professor, not a generic chatbot, and students are not locked into the vendor's data.

No public live URL. Status: under active development.

Year: 2025. Duration: ongoing. Role: full stack and AI engineer. Industry: EdTech. Category: AI product.

Stack: React, Node.js, PostgreSQL, OpenAI, RAG, vector database.

Client: Zoheb. PhD in Chemistry from Northwestern University, AI Fellow at Handshake, based in the United States, and close to production AI systems. He saw universities restricting or banning AI tools even among people who understand the technology.

The problem. Professors do not trust generic AI tools. Institutions worry about data privacy, misuse of student data, intellectual-property leakage, and vendor lock-in. Existing tools are not professor-centric and not built for institutional policy. The line used in the case study: professors do not want an AI tool, they want a trusted extension of themselves.

Design principles, treated as foundations rather than features:

- Privacy first
- Minimal data exposure
- No unnecessary logging
- No reuse of data for model training
- Institution-friendly architecture
- Professor-controlled AI behavior
- The product amplifies teaching; it does not replace it

Three fronts, with hard boundaries:

- Professor: configure and own the companion
- Student: chat-based learning
- Super admin: minimal visibility on purpose

What a professor can do:

- Upload lecture notes, PDFs, and readings
- Set behavior, tone, and teaching style
- Brand the companion with their name and course identity
- Share a unique course link
- Define what the AI is allowed to discuss
- Tune responses to a teaching philosophy

What a student gets. A familiar chat interface, deliberately plain:

- Questions tied to that course's materials
- Clarification outside class
- Revision at their own pace
- Help preparing for assessments
- A non-judgmental place to ask
- The professor's curated knowledge, not a general model wandering off-syllabus

Privacy measures:

- No unnecessary conversation logging
- No data crossing between courses
- No model training on student data
- Super admin cannot browse conversations or course content

What super admin is allowed to see: professor count and student count. What they are not allowed to see: conversations and course content. The restriction is the compliance story.

Who it is for. University professors, academic institutions, research-driven educators. Secondary: teaching assistants, departments, and private institutes with strict data policies.

Why it is considered harder than consumer AI: high-trust academic buyers, a requirement to balance capability with compliance, skeptical expert users, and no room for a black-box product.

Current state. Active development. Core architecture finalized. Privacy-first design locked. Early builds in progress. Positioned at the intersection of AI, education, privacy, and institutional trust. The ambition stated in the case study is to build AI systems people are comfortable trusting, not only using.

## Editorial case study (not a client build)

Separate from the six products, the site carries one long-form case study authored by BitB Labs, dated Jan 29, 2026, about 12 minutes, category recruitment, marked featured.

Title: How Generative AI Is Transforming High-Volume Hiring.

Subtitle: Industry education. The shift from manual hiring to AI-augmented recruitment.

Summary: How generative AI reshapes recruitment at scale, from resume screening to structured interviews. It is written as industry education and pattern-sharing. It describes a hiring team introducing generative AI as a co-pilot. It is not tied to a named BitBLabs client product in the data, though it matches the recruitment work done inside Natvoiz.

The argument.

High-volume hiring is where traditional recruitment breaks. Companies hiring hundreds or thousands of people a year still depend on manual effort and rough judgment, even with a modern applicant tracking system. Thousands of applications, pressure to close roles, candidate drop-off, and tired interviewers. Teams work harder. Outcomes do not improve in proportion.

The old workflow:

1. Job descriptions written or reused by hand, with vague skills.
2. Resumes screened by keyword, under time pressure.
3. Scheduling by email, with candidates dropping off.
4. Interviews and feedback that vary by interviewer, with slow decisions.

The hidden cost: recruiters processing noise. Speed traded against fairness, and fairness traded against speed. Rule-based automation (filters, templates, schedulers) cut effort and did not raise quality, because it cannot read context, transferable skills, or open-ended answers, and it cannot scale judgment.

The intervention. Generative AI inserted only where human judgment was overloaded. Principle: AI supports decisions. Humans make them.

Where it was placed:

- Resume understanding: skills, experience patterns, project depth, formats normalized into profiles. Recruiters read skill profiles, not raw resumes. Shortlists move from keywords to capability signals.
- Skill mapping against the role.
- Structured screening: role questions, scenarios, and behavioral prompts, delivered asynchronously so interview capacity grows without burning out interviewers.
- Response analysis: structured summaries, skill indicators, communication signals. Recruiters review insights, not raw recordings.
- Comparison views: strengths, risks, role fit. No final recommendation. The recruiter decides.
- Humans keep final shortlist, culture, offers, and candidate conversations.

The line used for the end state: the system did not remove humans. It removed chaos.

Outcomes described (awareness-level, across multiple high-volume roles):

- Resume screening time reduced 60–70%
- Recruiter capacity up 2–3x
- Feedback turnaround from days to minutes
- Interview consistency improved
- Quality improved rather than slipping

Lessons the piece wants a reader to keep:

- Generative AI works as a co-pilot, not as a replacement recruiter
- Skills-first evaluation beats keyword screening
- Structured AI interviews scale capacity without burnout
- Speed and fairness can exist together if AI is placed at the bottleneck
- Where AI sits in the workflow matters more than which model is used
- Human judgment stays the differentiator
- Structured roles and clear skills are a prerequisite
- Recruiters shift from screener, to evaluator, to decision-maker
- Decisions move from gut feel toward signals

Closing claim: companies that delay this shift will hire slower and hire worse. Judgment, not speed, is the competitive advantage. Generative AI absorbed the noise so recruiters could use judgment.

Case-study categories the site is set up to filter, even though only recruitment is populated: LLMs, AI Agents, Prompting, RAG, AI UX, Recruitment. The case-studies index describes itself as visual learning: deep dives into AI concepts, tools, and techniques through visual stories.

## Principles that repeat across the work

These are the ideas the portfolio keeps proving, useful as the studio's actual point of view:

- Build the platform, not the one-off. Natvoiz and Setoo both started from a single calling job and became configurable systems reused across workflows and clients.
- Put AI where judgment is overloaded, and leave the decision with a person. Hiring, financial explanations, and teaching all follow this.
- Non-engineers must be able to run it. Flow builders, CSV import, role-based dashboards, guided financial workflows, professor-controlled course links.
- Separate the surfaces. Admin, operator, and end user do not share one interface.
- Privacy and audit are product decisions. Course Companion hides content from its own super admin. Natvoiz keeps recordings, transcripts, and org isolation because enterprises require it.
- Domain accuracy beats model novelty. Axion Plan's value is a correct financial model with AI on top. DigiProPass's value is a scoring and passport workflow that matches a regulation. Healthy Fasal's value is an operating model (demand, then procurement) encoded in software.
- Latency and operations are part of the product. A voice bot at 2 seconds is a different product from one at 300 milliseconds. A grocery platform that cannot route, challan, and reconcile is not a supply chain.
